The Vibecoders Are Coming For Us

AI Coding Vibecoding Perspective Source: coz.is — April 2026

Oh man, the past few months were crazy. I went from extremely exhilarated to complete tragedy (in case you're living under a rock, I'm referring to AI coding)

The past few years I was completely unimpressed by the code generated by ChatGPT & Co, but since last December I found Claude Code incredibly more useful, and in February I notice a big jump in its capabilities. At the time I was working on a codebase with a great test suite, which greatly increases the effectiveness of AI agents as it allows them to iteratively improve on their output. I felt like I found a loophole in the matrix. It was great.

But then... I looked around and found that everyone was having that exact feeling. Everyone just hacked the matrix? From that moment onward it was complete doom. Were the effects not a consequence of my hard earned skill? Was the success I was having just Claude Code?

Even though I'm generally conservative about technology and wait until it proves its usefulness before I jump in, I like to keep my mind open. Ever since vibecoding first became a thing I followed its developments and the new communities that rise around it. I saw it as a cool phenomenon that opened new possibilities, but would probably not replace "hardcore programming". As I was saying, around February something completely unexpected happened, and I was forced to reassess all my assumptions. All my senior hardcore programmer friends and people I followed online were jumping ship. Most of them didn't write more than a couple lines of code in months. The FOMO was crazy. Was I going to be an electrician after all?

I decided that I needed to simply understand what was going on, follow new developments while keeping my mind open, and wait for the situation to stabilize.

Okay. We wrote all the apps. What do we do now?

I was dying. Of course people with no background in software are only really aware of websites and applications. They could generate whatever but don't even know what to ask. That completely changed my perspective. Turns out, knowledge is still key!

At the same time the first posts mentioning performance and security issues started popping out. One person paid a professional software developer to vet their app, which ended up pointing out a few minor issues that could be easily fixed. The author of the post appeared to feel extremely satisfied with that review!

I also noticed that working on projects I didn't understand deeply didn't really work. I always ended up going back and studying the underlying solution I needed to build. If you want any degree of control over what you're generating you need to understand it. But the cool thing is, you can get a lot more out of your understanding than before! You can do so much more with less information!

Regardless, I wanted to say that I am extremely happy that so many people are becoming passionate about building software. I suspect that by the end vibecoders will become more tradcoders than the other way around, and it's very motivating that what I learned up to now may become useful to even more people!

Happy vibecoding!
